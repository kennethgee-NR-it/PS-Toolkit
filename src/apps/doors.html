    At the door

    Simulate

    Pick something that happens at the door. Watch where current flows, what the lock does, and what the hub reports. Anything surprising here is cheaper to fix now than on site.

    Wiring

    Red: + power Black: − power, ground Green: signal + White: signal − (COM) Cat6, T568B Moving amber: current flowing

    Reading the schematic. The relay inside the hub is a switch. Its lever always touches COM and rests on NC; when the hub unlocks the door it swings to NO. In powered mode the hub feeds its own 12V into that lever; in dry mode the lever carries nothing until an external supply is wired to COM.

    Wire colors. Red and black carry power, green and white carry signal. Each device gets its own cable, so colors repeat from cable to cable; label both ends. When a black conductor has to carry + (the return leg of a cable looped through a push-to-exit, for example), re-mark it with red tape at both ends.

          Glossary

          Fail-safe
          A lock that needs power to stay locked. If power is cut, it unlocks. Maglocks are always fail-safe. Used where people must be able to get out, such as egress doors.
          Fail-secure
          A lock that needs power to unlock. If power is cut, it stays locked. Most electric strikes are fail-secure; the inside lever still opens the door mechanically.
          COM, NO, NC
          The three terminals of a relay. COM is the common connection. NC (normally closed) is connected to COM at rest. NO (normally open) connects to COM only when the relay switches.
          Powered (wet) output
          The hub supplies the lock’s voltage itself: 12V DC, up to 1A. Fewest wires, but 12V locks only, and the lock loses power whenever the hub does.
          Dry output
          A relay with no power of its own; it only opens and closes a circuit. An external supply provides the voltage. Needed for 24V locks, high-current locks, and doors that must run on a battery-backed supply.
          REX
          Request to exit. A button or motion sensor on the secure side that tells the hub someone is leaving, so it unlocks without raising an alarm.
          DPS
          Door position sensor, a magnetic contact on the frame. It tells the hub whether the door is open, so it can report forced and held-open doors.
          Fire alarm relay
          A contact from the fire alarm panel wired in series with a fail-safe lock. It opens during an alarm and releases the door no matter what the hub is doing.
          Voltage drop
          The voltage lost in the cable on the way to the lock. Longer runs and thinner wire lose more. Aim for at least 90% of the lock’s rated voltage at the lock.
          Diode or MOV
          A small part across the lock’s terminals that absorbs the voltage spike a lock coil makes when it switches off. Protects the hub’s relay contacts.
          AHJ
          Authority having jurisdiction: the fire marshal or building inspector who decides what the code requires for a door.

          Ratings quoted for the hub come from Ubiquiti’s published UA-Hub specs; confirm them for your hub model. Code notes reflect common US practice; the AHJ has the final say.
